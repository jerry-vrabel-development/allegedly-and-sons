# 0016 — The cast

- **Status:** Draft. The host is built; every other character here is a brief
  in words, with no drawing and no code yet
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Author:** Jerry Vrabel

## Problem

The show has one face. The host ([0013](0013-host-and-artists.md)) sells from
the stage, strangers ring in ([0014](0014-front-page-show.md)), and nobody
else works there. A shopping channel needs a cast: someone to hold the item
up, someone to contradict the host, someone to value the goods and someone to
deliver them.

This note is the cast sheet. It is the writer's seat for each character
(0013: every piece has a writer's seat and an artist's seat), written as the
brief we would hand a partner, so it doubles as the first set of briefs for
submissions ([0017](0017-submissions.md)).

## What the cast is (decided 2026-11-14)

- **The cast is the show's own people.** They recur, they have names and
  jobs, and each can be drawn. Callers are not cast: a caller is a stranger
  who rings once (0014).
- **Otherworldly, and different from each other.** Each character comes from
  a different world or dimension, with its own body, its own way of talking
  and its own idea of what is normal. No two are the same kind of being.
- **Only the host says he is human.** Everyone else is plainly what they
  are. He stands among them insisting it is a perfectly ordinary staff. That
  is his joke, and the cast makes it funnier.
- **Otherworldly means invented.** No character borrows a real culture's
  dress, accent, name or features as its "alien" trait, and none is a
  stand-in for a real person (0009, 0013).
- **The act is for the audience** (0013). Every character plays the
  hard-sell black market out front. None of them is ever the voice of what a
  partner or a buyer relies on: prices, terms, refunds and the tally are
  said plainly, by the page.

## The cast

Names are decided: Guy Normal, Trixie Parallax, Ibid, Loupe and Nickel. The Courier
has none, and Gerald is Gerald.

### The host

Built; see 0013. A game-show host, used-car salesman and black-market
pitchman at once, and, allegedly, a perfectly ordinary human being.

- **Job in the act:** oversells everything.
- **Name:** Guy Normal.

### The Showgirl, the host's assistant

- **Job in the act:** presents the exhibit. She carries it out, holds it up
  and turns it to the light as if it were a crown, whatever it is.
- **From:** a dimension where applause is the currency and display is the
  language.
- **What she is:** a glamorous plumed being. The feathers of her headdress
  and train are her own, she has more arms than the gesture needs, all of
  them graceful, and she knows exactly how good she looks.
- **Her type:** the bubbly, flirty, delightfully dim showgirl. She calls
  everyone "hon", flirts with the host, the camera and the exhibit alike,
  mangles the long words, loses track of what the thing is mid-presentation
  and is thrilled by all of it. Her sparkle never dims.
- **How she talks:** brightly and cheerfully wrong. "It's a, like,
  authenticated, um, antique?" Her plumage still says what she really thinks:
  it flares for an exhibit she rates and droops for one she doesn't.
- **Her joke:** the audience loves her more than the host, and the host
  can't work out why. Her wild guesses keep turning out to be nearly right,
  which the Archivist finds unbearable.
- **Parts that move:** plumage (flare, droop), the presenting arms, a slow
  turn of the exhibit, a wink, a hair toss.
- **Where she appears:** the stage, beside the inset; exhibit pages.
- **Never:** explicit. She is glamorous and flirtatious in costume, plumes
  and attitude, with no nudity, and the joke is on the exhibits and the host,
  never on her. She never speaks for prices, terms or the tally.
- **Name:** Trixie Parallax.

### The Archivist

- **Job in the act:** undersells what the host oversells. He states what
  the object is.
- **From:** the Stacks, a dimension with two directions, alphabetical and
  chronological.
- **What he is:** a tall, grey, narrow being with four arms (file, stamp,
  hold the tea, adjust the glasses) and three eyes in a column behind
  half-moon lenses. Sleeve garters and a visor.
- **How he talks:** flat and exact. "It is a paperclip." He cannot say a
  thing that isn't accurate, so the host talks over him.
- **His joke:** he is right and it never helps.
- **Parts that move:** the stamping arm, the glasses arm, one eye of the
  three blinking out of turn, a page turning.
- **Where he appears:** the stage, at a small desk on the far side from the
  host; exhibit pages.
- **Never:** the voice of the page's real facts. He is a lore character who
  happens to be accurate; the ticker's Fact lines and the Fine Print's
  "Plainly" lines stay the page's own.
- **Name:** Ibid.

### The Appraiser

- **Job in the act:** values what can't be valued. Runs the Vault.
- **From:** a dimension where everything has exactly one price and nobody is
  allowed to know it.
- **What it is:** one large eye with a jeweller's loupe, afloat, with two
  small hands and a very small waistcoat.
- **How it talks:** in ranges. "Between nothing and everything." "Priceless,
  in that I cannot price it."
- **Its joke:** total confidence and no numbers.
- **Parts that move:** the loupe swinging in, the pupil narrowing, the
  hands turning an object over, a slow bob.
- **Where it appears:** the Vault and Vault cards.
- **Never:** a real appraiser, museum or valuation (0003). It never states a
  price or a value in money, and it never comments on the Vault's posted odds.
- **Name:** Loupe.

### The Courier

- **Job in the act:** delivers. Nobody asks the Courier questions.
- **From:** a dimension where distance is optional.
- **What it is:** never seen whole. It is drawn half out of a wormhole: a
  long coat, a satchel, some legs, and more legs than that the next time.
- **How it talks:** it doesn't. It hands over a delivery slip, and the slip
  says very little.
- **Its joke:** it has sometimes arrived before it left.
- **Parts that move:** the wormhole's rim, the satchel's swing, a slip held
  out, legs withdrawing.
- **Where it appears:** Dispatch emails, the thank-you page, the Fine
  Print's flavor.
- **Never:** a promise about shipping. Real ship times and refunds are said
  plainly in the Fine Print; the Courier is only its flavor.
- **Name:** none. Asking is discouraged.

### Nickel

- **Job in the act:** the wisecracking sidekick. He cracks jokes, narrates the
  ticker and cannot let a silence go by.
- **From:** a dimension of nothing but transactions, where every sentence
  closes a sale.
- **What he is:** a brass cash-register robot with spindly arms, a bell on his
  head and a drawer in his belly that bangs open on a punchline. His mouth
  is a slot, and the joke comes out on a strip of ticker tape.
- **How he talks:** constantly, in puns and patter. "That one's on the house.
  Everything else is on the Bureau." He laughs at his own jokes first, and
  the bell rings for him.
- **His joke:** he is the only one who truly understands what things cost,
  and he cannot keep it to himself, so everyone else has stopped asking him.
- **Parts that move:** the bell, the tape feeding out, the drawer slamming
  open, the arms flung wide at a punchline.
- **Where he appears:** the stage's ticker, where his tape is the Lore lines,
  and the stage beside the Archivist.
- **Never:** the voice of the page's facts. He tells the Lore; the Fact lines
  and the Fine Print's "Plainly" lines stay the page's own. He never states
  a real price, and he is not modelled on any existing character: the
  cash-register body, the tape and the bell are his own.
- **Name:** Nickel.

### Gerald

Already in the shop's copy: certificates are "witnessed by Gerald, the
Bureau's notary. Gerald is not a notary."

- **Job in the act:** witnesses things.
- **From:** the dimension next door. He commutes.
- **What he is:** a small, mild being of clear jelly in a tiny tie. The
  stamp he uses is visible inside him between uses.
- **How he talks:** politely, and mostly to say what he is not.
- **His joke:** he is not a notary, and he has never claimed to be. Everyone
  else keeps saying it.
- **Parts that move:** a wobble, the stamp rising through him, the tie
  straightening.
- **Where he appears:** certificates.
- **Never:** a real notarisation, seal or legal witness. The line "Gerald is
  not a notary" stays wherever he does.

## The brief every drawing shares

From 0013, so a partner's version can replace a house sketch:

- **An original SVG drawing** on a 400 by 500 canvas, in the stage's
  colours: near-black ink, cream, oxblood and gold, plus one colour of the
  character's own.
- **One file per character,** holding the drawing, the artist's credit and
  the names of the parts that move.
- **Named moving parts,** prefixed with the character, the way the host's
  are (`host-jaw`, `host-arm`).
- **Drawn still.** Motion belongs to the effects group, stops with the
  "Motion" toggle and never starts under reduced motion.
- **A short description** for screen readers, and nothing a visitor needs
  that is said only by the drawing.

## Order of work

1. **This note:** the briefs, agreed.
2. **One sketch per PR,** house art credited to "The Bureau", starting with
   the Archivist: he goes on the stage that is already built.
3. **Place each character** where the brief says, one page at a time.

## Open questions

- Whether the Showgirl is the right title for the page, or only the trope
  she plays. "The host's assistant" is the alternative.
- Whether the cast speaks on the stage (a line each per segment, like the
  callers) or is only seen. Nickel is the natural first voice: his tape
  would carry the ticker's Lore lines.
- Whether more cast is wanted: a bandleader, an announcer, security at the
  back door.
- How cast work earns for partners. It is not a product, so it falls under
  the open question in 0013.

## What would change our mind

- A character who reads as a real-world stereotype to anyone: redraw or
  rewrite them.
- A partnered writer or artist with a better character: the sheet is house
  work and is meant to be replaced.
