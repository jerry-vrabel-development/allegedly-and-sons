# 0017 — How writers and artists submit work

- **Status:** Proposed. Nothing is open and nothing is built
- **Date:** 2026-11-14
- **Author:** Jerry Vrabel

## Problem

[0002](0002-writers.md) and [0013](0013-host-and-artists.md) say why the shop
wants human writers and artists, how they are credited and that they earn
when their exhibit sells. Neither says how work arrives: what we ask for,
where it is sent, who decides, what a partner hears back and when anything
goes live.

Without a process we can't invite anyone, and a clumsy one would break the
promise in 0013: the act is for the audience, and partners are dealt with
fairly and plainly.

## What a submission is

- **Against a brief.** Every open seat has a written brief: an exhibit's
  story or art, a character from the cast sheet ([0016](0016-the-cast.md)),
  callers, wire bulletins, Vault cards. A partner submits for a named seat.
- **One craft or both.** A writer, an artist, or the two as a team.
- **Human-made.** The partner's own work. What that allows is an open
  question below, and it must be answered before opening.

## Two ways to take submissions

### Option A — By email, reviewed by hand

A public page explains the program and links the open briefs. A partner
sends work to a submissions address on the shop's domain. Jerry reviews it.
Records are kept in a private list.

- **For:** no code beyond one page. It can open as soon as the agreement
  exists. It is personal, which suits the first few partners.
- **Against:** it doesn't scale past a few dozen submissions. Tracking is
  by hand. Attachments arrive in every format.

### Option B — A form and a review queue on the site

A submission form on the site, files kept in private storage, and a review
queue behind the Bureau's sign-in, beside the Ledger.

- **For:** one format, one place, a status for every submission, and it
  scales.
- **Against:** real work to build and to secure. It takes uploads from
  strangers and holds their contact details, so it needs limits, scanning
  and care in logs. It would be built before we know anyone will submit.

## Choice

**Option A first, then Option B when the volume earns it.** The first
partners are few and worth talking to. What we learn from handling them by
hand is what the form should ask.

## The process (Option A)

1. **Find the brief.** The program page lists open seats. Each brief says
   what is wanted, the size or length, the lines the work must keep, and
   what it earns.
2. **Send the work** to the submissions address, naming the seat, with a
   pen name or a real one, and a statement that it is the sender's own work.
3. **Acknowledged within a few days,** by a person, in plain words.
4. **Reviewed against the brief and the shop's lines:** outlandish, never
   plausible; no real people; nothing that fakes activity or a price
   (0003, 0009, 0014). For art, that it can be shown and, if wanted, moved.
5. **An answer either way:** accepted, declined, or "close, here is what
   would change our mind". Never silence. A declined piece stays the
   partner's and is deleted from our records on request.
6. **Agreement before anything is published.** The partner sees and signs
   the plain-words agreement (0002, 0013): what we may publish and adapt,
   for how long, what they keep, and what they earn.
7. **Published with credit:** a themed title, a bio page if they want one,
   and only what they have approved.
8. **Paid as agreed,** on verified sales ([0001](0001-analytics.md)).

## Lines the process keeps

- **Plain words for partners.** The program page, the briefs, every reply
  and the agreement are written plainly. Flavor may sit beside them and
  never replaces them (0013).
- **Private by default.** Submissions and contact details are stored
  privately, never in logs or analytics (0002), and are seen only by the
  reviewer.
- **Nothing goes live without approval** from the shop and the partner.
- **Unaccepted work is not used.** Not as a prompt, not as a starting point,
  not later. If the shop independently has something similar in progress,
  the reply says so.
- **No fee to submit,** and no purchase required.

## Before this can open

All of these are open questions in 0002 or 0013. The process is ready when
they are answered:

- [ ] The agreement is written, and a lawyer has reviewed it.
- [ ] The split is decided: the shop's share, the writer's and the artist's,
      and what it is cut from.
- [ ] What work that isn't a product earns (cast, callers, cards).
- [ ] What counts as human-made, and how a submission says so.
- [ ] How payouts are made (leading option: Stripe Connect) and how a
      refunded sale is handled.
- [ ] Which formats we can take beyond SVG, and who prepares art for motion.
- [ ] A submissions address on the shop's domain.
- [ ] The program page and the first briefs are written.

## Open questions

- Who is invited first: open submissions, invitations, local Chicago
  artists and writers, or a mix (0002, 0013).
- Whether a partner must be an adult, and how that is confirmed.
- Whether more than one person may hold a seat at once (two stories for one
  exhibit), and how a later version replaces an earlier one.
- How long we keep declined submissions before deleting them unasked.

## What would change our mind

- More than a handful of submissions a week, or attachments we can't open:
  build Option B.
- A partner who would rather work through a form: the same.
